A sofa can look tired long before it has reached the end of its useful life. Small problems such as loose joints, flattened foam or worn fabric are often repairable, while a strong frame can make restoration a better choice than replacement.

Start with the frame

The frame is the foundation of your sofa. Sit in different places and listen for creaks, movement or a noticeable lean. Check underneath for cracked timber, loose joints or a sagging base. A solid frame with localised damage is usually a good candidate for repair.

A useful rule of thumb

If the frame is structurally sound and the sofa still fits your room comfortably, repairing the seating and upholstery can often deliver much better value than buying new.

Check the comfort layer

Flattened cushions do not always mean the whole sofa is finished. Foam, webbing and springs can lose resilience over time, but these components can be replaced or repaired. Compare the height of each cushion and look for permanent dips after the sofa has been left unused.

  • Cushions feel uneven or collapse quickly
  • Seats have lost their original height
  • Springs feel noisy, loose or uncomfortable
  • The sofa is still the right size and shape for your space

Look at the upholstery

Fabric fading, loose stitching and small tears are common signs of use, not automatic reasons to replace a sofa. Reupholstery can change the look completely, while a tailored loose cover can be a practical option for homes with children, pets or frequent spills.

When replacement makes more sense

Replacement may be worth considering when the frame has extensive damage, the sofa no longer suits the room, or the cost of restoring several major components approaches the cost of a better-quality new piece. An inspection gives you a clearer answer than judging by appearance alone.

A professional inspection can identify what is repairable, what should be replaced and which finish will suit your everyday use.
